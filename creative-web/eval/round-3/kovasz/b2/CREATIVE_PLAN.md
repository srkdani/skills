# Kovász sample, night bench

Deal salt r3b. Worlds dealt: Traveling object, Walk into the place, Bench in stages. Motion dealt: Spring. Picture dealt: Ink and one color.

## Harvest

Facts only this bakery has. Sensory and physical items are marked.

1. The bake starts at 11pm and runs while the city is asleep.
2. The door opens at 6am.
3. The starter is 41 years old.
4. The starter was carried from a grandmother's village, not bought as a packet.
5. The basket sells through by noon and is not refilled that day.
6. Flour dust sits on the maple bench and sticks to wet hands. Physical, touch and sight.
7. The starter smells sharp, green apple and warm grain. Smell.
8. The lump is cool at midnight and hot off the deck at dawn. Temperature.
9. A lame, a razor on a handle, cuts the skin before the bake. Tool.
10. Banneton coils print rings into the floured skin. Sight and touch.
11. At 3am the oven mouth is the only warm light in the shop. Light.
12. The crust crackles as it cools on the rack. Sound.
13. A fold ends in a wet slap of dough on wood. Sound.
14. Kovász is the Hungarian word for the starter itself, so the shop is named for the jar.
15. The shop is in Budapest. The village is not named, and the page will not invent one.
16. By noon the peel is bare and the bench keeps a flour ring where the basket sat. Sight.
17. Opening the oven throws a burst of steam. Sight, sound, heat.

No prices were given. None will be invented.

## Truths

1. The bench starts at 11pm and the same lump is what the door sells at 6am. Rival: a hotel breakfast bakery. They plate a buffet after dawn and do not keep one lump on one bench all night. They do not have this in this form.
2. The starter is 41 years old and was carried from a grandmother's village. Rival: a supermarket in-store bakery. They feed a commercial culture from a packet. They do not have this jar.
3. The basket is empty by noon and is not refilled. Rival: an all-day cafe bakery. They bake a second round after lunch and the basket comes back. They do not end the day at the flour ring.

## Pick

The night bench (concept 1).

The visitor scrolls one lump through the night and sees it spring, then sees the flour ring. That is the sentence they say afterwards. The jar and the peel stay objects on that bench.

Rejected worlds, in writing:

- Traveling object. The starter did come from a village, but the memory is the lump changing on one bench, not a figure crossing a map. A traveler over a menu would be the failure that deal names: the object decorating a hero while the hours sit in cards.
- Walk into the place. The harvest has no shop interior to stand in, only the bench, the jar, the oven mouth, and the empty basket. Three rooms would invent a storefront, and a darkened picture with a headline is the failure that deal names.

Motion is kept: spring, because oven spring is the bake's one bounce. Picture rule is kept: ink and one color, because flour and char are already a drawing. Neither fights a harvest fact.

## Visual system

- Material the page is made of: a maple bench dusted with flour, because the night's work happens on that floured wood and the lump is the thing that changes.
- Light source: one baker's lamp at 11pm, a bright pool on the flour and the rest of the shop dark, because they start when the street is dark and the oven is still shut. The lamp picks the brightness. The page is not a daytime cafe and not a black void.
- Palette, from that material:
  - `#1C1612` ink, because the baker's pencil and the char on the crust are the same dark.
  - `#E4D3B0` dough fill, because the lump under the lamp is flour-pale dough.
  - `#3E3228` bench, because oiled maple at night is brown-black outside the lamp.
  - `#F6EBD4` lamp pool, because flour directly under the bulb is the brightest patch. It is a pool on the bench, with crumb holes and flour specks, not a flat cream field.
  - `#7A542C` peel wood, because the peel is a drier, redder board than the bench.
- Type: display "Alfa Slab One", file from `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Alfa+Slab+One&family=Newsreader:opsz,wght@6..72,500;6..72,600&display=swap`, because the name is stamped into the crust the way a bakery stamps a bag, blunt and heavy. Body "Newsreader" from the same file, because the bench note and the grandmother's card are a recipe you read with flour on your fingers. Display 108px at 1440 wide, body 18px, so display is 6 times the body. At 390 wide, display 72px and body 18px, still 4 times. Tracking on the display is -0.03em, because the slab can take a tight stamp.
- Picture rule: ink and one color, kept. Stroke 1.75px, round caps, ink `#1C1612`, fill `#E4D3B0`, a displacement wobble of scale 3 (inside 2 to 4). No gradients as shading. The picture depicts the bench, the lump, the jar, the banneton, the lame, and the bare basket.
- Motion: spring, `back.out(1.4)`, 0.45s, once, because dough springs in the oven and then stops. A stage change overshoots once. Menus, cuts, and feed taps are instant or under 150ms. Nothing else bounces.
- Sound: none. The crackle is real, and a toggle would be a second idea. Never autoplay.
- Grade: one picture treatment. Baker's lamp on flour, drawings in one ink and one dough fill. Name in the page as `GRADE: baker's lamp on flour, one ink #1C1612 and one dough fill #E4D3B0`. The room's bench and peel are wood, not a second grade. A village photograph does not exist, so the jar carries an ink box marked placeholder.

## Rhythm

1. Bench: one large lump, one side note, the peel in the corner. The object owns the viewport.
2. Jar: one tall vessel, three feed words as rings on the glass. A column of glass, not a lump.
3. Score: one wide skin, three cuts written across it. Low and horizontal.
4. Shelf: one rail, three hung labels (hours, Budapest, flour ring). Short, almost no sentence.

No section is heading, then text, then a card grid. None of these four can swap places and survive on words alone.

## Removal

A looping steam puff over the oven mouth. It will be built with the spring stage, then deleted. Steam is weather. The concept is the lump changing on the bench, and the motion deal is one spring, then stop. A loop fights both.

## Genericness

Similar brief: a neighborhood sourdough bakery that opens early and talks about an old starter. For them this plan would have slipped into a flour-colored field, a serif headline, and three loaves in a row. That part is changed. The page is one lump on a night bench, advanced by hour, drawn in one ink and one dough fill, and it ends as a flour ring at noon. The lamp pool stays a patch on dark wood, with crumb holes, so it is not a cream theme. The three loaves are not drawn, because this shop sells one night's bake and then stops.

Batch 1 gate: The grey bench still carries one lump through the night, from a slack oval at 11pm to a flour ring at noon, and the narrow reflow was fixed so the headline, the hours, and the peel no longer cover each other.

Batch 2 gate: The night bench is now one ink drawing of the jar, the lump, and the basket, and scroll springs that same lump from slack to risen to scored to a flour ring, after the stage groups were actually hidden.

Batch 3 gate: The same bench now has two closer views, a wide skin you score with a tap and a tall jar you feed in order, and a wrong tap still tells you the jar is sulking.

Batch 4 gate: The bench still leads, and the small marks are the live peel status, the 41 on the jar, a flick of the lame, the flour ring in the footer, and a placeholder where the village photo is missing.

Batch 5 gate: The bench still shows one lump, reduced motion steps the hour without a pin, the narrow screen keeps the jar and the bowl in view, and the steam loop is gone so the only spring is the dough.
