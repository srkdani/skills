# Moth Night, sample

Deal salt r3. Three worlds were dealt. One is kept.

## Harvest

Facts only this night has. Sensory ones are marked.

1. The gallery lamps go off for a late opening, and the oak cabinets stay brown. They do not turn into a black room. (light)
2. UV torches are handed at the cloak desk and aimed by hand. The beam hits one drawer, not the whole hall. (light, touch)
3. After a few minutes the torch body is warm from the palm. (temperature)
4. Some moth hindwings take a dull sulphur under longwave UV, and the scale powder comes off on a finger. (sight, touch)
5. Calcite in the mineral cabinets is grey at noon and salmon under that same torch. (sight)
6. The entomology drawers smell of camphor and old manila labels. (smell)
7. The mineral hall is colder than the entomology room: stone floor, glass, brass pulls. (temperature, touch)
8. A cello plays unamplified between cabinets 14 and 15. The stone gives the bow a long decay. (sound)
9. Rosin dust settles on the stone under the chair. (sight)
10. A specimen label is typed on manila and pinned under the insect with a brass pin through the thorax. (material, process)
11. The day labels stay on the pins. The night does not hang a new wall panel. (ritual)
12. A cotton sheet hangs by the east door so moths have somewhere to land. (place, tool)
13. Visitors say "the calcite went pink" and "the cello was between the cabinets." (customers' words)
14. White phone lamps are the enemy. They wash the fluorescence out. Only the UV torch is asked for. (enemy)
15. The cloak desk punches a torch number on the night plate, and the torch comes back before the lamps do. (ritual)
16. A brass drawer pull is heavy. The drawer gives a short distance and then stops. (touch, sound)
17. Sample hours: lamps off at 19:00, torches back by 23:00, Sample hall. Moths are a dusk animal. (time)
18. Vocabulary of the night: unit tray, locality, thorax, longwave, rosin, cloak desk, cabinet.

## Truths

Truth 1. Moths and calcite share one unit tray, and the UV torch is what makes the hindwing and the calcite take the same salmon correction. A gem shop has minerals and no moth sheet. A butterfly house has insects under day lamps, not a UV torch on calcite. Rival: a butterfly house.

Truth 2. The cello is unamplified between cabinets 14 and 15, so it is written on the tray as a specimen label, not as a stage bill. A recital hall has a cello and a printed programme on a stage, not a cabinet gap and a pin. Rival: a cello recital.

Truth 3. The day gallery lamps are the enemy. The same typed labels stay, and the local hour decides whether the torches are out or the paper is still the day grey. An evening concert dims lights for mood and does not keep the day's specimen labels as the programme. Rival: an evening concert.

## Emotion

Hush.

"I pulled the tray and watched the salmon stamp cross a moth and a piece of calcite, then nick the cello label."

## Concepts

The site is a unit-tray label where the night programme is pins on one card and pulling the card stamps a UV correction across the moth and the calcite. Rival: a butterfly house. Their insects are not a stamped tray beside calcite, and they have no cello pin. Keep.

The site is the gap between cabinets 14 and 15 where the cello is the only soft object and the listing is the cabinet numbers. Rival: a recital hall. A cabinet gap does not fit them. Keep.

The site is a torch handover where the shutter is the drawer lip and each stop wakes one specimen. Rival: a cave torch tour. A drawer lip and a pinned specimen do not fit a cave. Keep.

## Pick

The unit-tray label. The visitor pulls that card and sees the salmon stamp hit the moth, the calcite, and then the cello label, which is the sentence they say afterwards. The cabinet gap and the handover are not added on. The cello and the hour are lines on this same label.

Rejected worlds, in writing:

Bench in stages is rejected. Moth Night does not build an object through workshop stages. A stage row would also fight that world's own ban on a scroller of steps, and it would drop the handled plate.

Clock in the room is rejected. A timepiece at 40 percent of the viewport would replace the plate. The picture rule wants a crop, a punch, and a serial, not a clock face. The hour stays as small print and a pencil tick on the label (truth 3). That is a fact on the document, not a second world.

Motion is accepted: Heavy, `power4.inOut`, 0.9 to 1.4s, travel under 80px, because the drawer is oak and brass and stops short. Not used on ticks or other repeated controls. Those are instant or under 150ms.

Picture rule is accepted: plate and stamp. Thin rules, stamped numbers, one correction color only on marks and the main action. The card looks handled: one cropped corner, one punch hole, one serial. Not a hairline grid on every edge.

## Visual system

Material the page is made of: a manila unit-tray card lying on an oak cabinet, because the specimens are pinned to aged paper in an oak drawer and the visitor is holding that card.

Light source: the UV torch, because that is the lamp in the brief. It is aimed at the paper, so the card is the lit object and the oak around it is the unlit hall. The oak is dim brown, because the lamps are off and the wood is still wood. Fluorescence is printed as the correction stamp, because a lamp bloom would be a second light and the picture rule is a stamp.

Palette, five hex values from those materials:

- Manila paper `#E6D3A8`, because the unit-tray labels are aged manila, not a cream theme.
- Oak `#5A3E2E`, because the cabinet is oak seen with the gallery lamps off, dim and still brown.
- Brass `#A68542`, because the pins, the pull, and the drawer hardware are brass.
- Iron-gall ink `#241C16`, because the locality lines are typed and then penned in dark ink.
- Correction `#8E2F48`, because calcite goes salmon under the UV torch, and that dried salmon is the only stamp pad. It is used only on the glowing marks and on the main action.

Type: display face Alfa Slab One, file `fonts/alfa-slab-one-latin.woff2`, taken from `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Alfa+Slab+One&family=Courier+Prime:wght@400;700&display=swap`, because the night name is rubber-stamped onto the tray card with a heavy slab, not engraved and not italic. Text face Courier Prime, files `fonts/courier-prime-400-latin.woff2` and `fonts/courier-prime-700-latin.woff2` from that same URL, because locality labels are typed on the museum typewriter. Display size 104px, body size 18px, because 104 is more than four times 18, so the stamp dwarfs the typed line. Tracking on the stamp `-0.03em`, because the slab can take a tight lockup on the card. Small print on the card is 13px, because a real label's serial is small and still readable.

Picture rule: plate and stamp, kept, because the artifact is a handled tray card. The picture depicts, in plan view, a brass pin through a geometric moth, a chipped calcite rhomb, a geometric cello reduced to scroll and bouts, an empty pin hole, a punch, and a serial. No perspective animals.

Motion: Heavy, kept. `power4.inOut`, 0.9 to 1.4s, object travel under 80px, because a full drawer stops after a short pull. Scroll scrub on the signature stays tied to the hand (scrub about 0.6) so the visitor is not waiting on a curve. The Aim control runs the same pull to the end in 1.1s with `power4.inOut`. Repeated ticks do not use that curve.

Sound: opt-in and off by default, because a cello is in the hall and nothing may start by itself. One bow transient, under 200ms, only after a tap on the cello label. No autoplay.

Grade: one treatment for the whole page. Salmon correction on manila, one ink. `GRADE:` names it. No second grade, no glow wash, no mixed photos. Missing photographs stay as drawn stand-ins on the card and are marked sample.

## Budget

Every idea names a truth.

Big, exactly one. Truth 1. Technique 20, a scroll and drag scrub of the stamp sequence. Element `#uv-stamp` with `data-tier="big"` and `data-truth="1"`. Visible in the first viewport. The brass pull is the touch path, a range at least 44px, travel 64px. The Aim stamp runs the same progress.

Storyboard, scroll and touch in the same beats:

- 0 to 30% of the scrub: the pull moves about 20px of its 64px and the moth hindwing takes the salmon stamp in place, because truth 1. Touch: the same pull, the same threshold.
- 30 to 70%: the calcite rhomb takes the stamp. The moth stamp stays. The drawer lip shifts only 12px, because the drawer is heavy. Truth 1.
- 70 to 100%: a small nick of the same ink lands on the cello label, because truth 2, the cello is a line on this tray, not a glowing body.
- The headline, Aim the torch, and the hours stay in the first view. Scroll scrubs the presses as the card moves, with no extra pin, because a hold left an empty stretch of manila. The pull sets the same progress directly. Aim the torch runs that progress to the end in 1.1s with `power4.inOut` and does not move the page.
- Reduced motion: no pin and no travel. The pull steps through the three presses. Aim the torch sets the last press without sliding.

Medium, two. They share technique 19 (a control that belongs to the document) and technique 28 (the local hour). No third technique.

- Medium 1, truth 3. `#shift-ticks`, pencil ticks for Day lamps, Handover, and Moth night. On load, the local hour selects one: 19:00 to 05:59 moth night, 17:00 to 18:59 handover, otherwise day lamps. The status line changes with the tick. Touch: each tick is at least 44px and answers in under 150ms. `data-live="time"`.
- Medium 2, truth 2. `#pin-swap`, tap the moth, the calcite, or the cello and the locality margin rewrites to that pin's line. The cello is read the same way as a specimen, because truth 2. Touch: tap the pin. The swap answers in under 150ms.

Small, six. Quick, under 300ms, off the headline and off the Aim stamp.

- Small, truth 3. `#shift-line`, the live sentence of the current shift. `data-live="time"`.
- Small, truth 1. `#warm-line`, the loading line on the pull. It changes once the stamp has landed.
- Small, truth 1. `#punch`, a fidget. Tap the punch hole and it fills in under 100ms.
- Small, truth 1. `#empty-pin`, tap the vacant hole and the empty line appears.
- Small, truth 3. `#missing-drawer`, tap serial 000 and the missing-drawer line appears.
- Small, truth 2. `#cloak-footer`, the footer is the cloak desk, not a link row. `#bow-toggle` sits with the cello label: opt-in bow, default off.

At least one of these uses the hour. Marked `data-live="time"`. Sample data is marked sample.

## Rhythm

1. Tray face: one held card, stamp headline, four unequal pins, the pull. A picture.
2. Locality margin: ragged typewriter lines, no boxes, a single brass rule at the left.
3. Shift ticks: three pencil circles stacked down the card, words beside them, not above a paragraph.
4. Tear-off strip: one horizontal perforated serial row, the footer as the last typed line.

No zone matches another. None is a heading, then text, then a card grid. Words sit on the card.

## Removal

Build a wing-flutter loop: the moth wing polygons rotate a few degrees and back, repeating, once the moth is drawn. Delete it in batch 5. It does not serve the concept. The moth is pinned through the thorax. A flutter makes the tray a cartoon, and the motion that tells truth 1 is the short heavy stamp, not a flap. Leave the `REMOVED:` comment after the CSS is gone.

## Genericness

Similar brief: Aquarium After Dark, another night visit with a torch and animals that seem to glow.

For that brief this plan would have used a dark tank and a roaming beam. That part is changed. The scene stays the dealt document: a manila unit tray on oak, and the only fluorescence is a dried-calcite stamp on the pins. An aquarium cannot use a specimen serial, a cello between cabinets 14 and 15, or calcite. The dealt world stays. The page does not escape into a black field with one acid accent, and it does not escape into a cream editorial serif.

Batch 1 gate: The grey card is still the unit tray from the picked concept, and the three stamp boxes press on as the short pull moves, so truth 1 is a correction landing on the pins. The hours and Aim the torch stay on that card. The pull travel stays at 12px on the lip and 64px on the knob, which is the heavy stop, and the lower zones stay a ragged margin, a tick column, and a tear-off strip.

Batch 2 gate: The signature is now the drawn unit tray, and the salmon correction lands on the moth hindwing, the calcite rhomb, and a nick on the cello label, which is the visitor sentence. The calcite was redrawn as a rhomb with a chip after it read as a star, and the cello nick was moved off the words so truth 2 stays readable. The lower margin, ticks, and strip stay placeholders.

Batch 3 gate: The two mediums are controls on the same tray card. The pencil ticks follow the local hour and rewrite the shift line, which is truth 3, and tapping a pin rewrites the locality so the cello is read like a specimen, which is truth 2. The stamp was set to ignore taps so it no longer covers the pins.

Batch 4 gate: The smalls stay on the tray card and off the Aim stamp: the live shift line, the warming line on the pull, the punch, the empty hole, serial 000, the cloak footer, and the bow toggle. They still name the hour, the stamp, and the cello between the cabinets. The warming line sits beside the pull so it does not cover the action.

Batch 5 gate: The wing flutter is gone, so the moth stays pinned and the salmon presses are the motion that tells truth 1. Reduced motion keeps the drawing, drops the lip travel, and steps the pull. At 390 the hours, Aim the torch, and the four pins stay on the card. The empty hold was removed so the label stays one sheet.
