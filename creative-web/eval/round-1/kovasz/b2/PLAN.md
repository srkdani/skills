# Kovász sample plan

## Harvest

Facts only this bakery has:

1. The bake starts at 11pm, while Budapest is asleep.
2. The shop door opens at 6am.
3. The baskets are empty by noon. Selling out is the point of the night.
4. The starter is 41 years old.
5. That starter was carried from a grandmother's village, not cultured in the shop.
6. The name Kovász is the Hungarian word for the leaven itself. The brand is the jar.
7. The place is Budapest. No other city is in the brief.
8. At 11pm the lid comes off and the culture smells sour and yeasty. Physical.
9. A rubber band marks the level in the jar. When the band is too low, the culture has not doubled. Physical.
10. Shaped loaves sleep in bannetons. The coil leaves a spiral in the skin. Physical.
11. A lame cuts one score. In the oven the cut opens into an ear. Physical.
12. Between 4am and 6am the crust ticks as it cools on the rack. Sound and heat. Physical.
13. The bench is a flax couche. Flour sits in the weave. Physical.
14. One bare bulb lights the bench at 11pm. The street door stays shut. Light. Physical.
15. At 6am cold street air meets bread that is still hot. Temperature. Physical.
16. At noon the spiral is still floured and the loaf is gone. Physical.
17. The same culture is fed every night, so today's crumb holds yesterday's jar.
18. The enemy is a loaf still on the rack after noon.

At least the smell, the band, the spiral, the ear, the ticking crust, the floured linen, the bulb, the cold door, and the empty basket are physical or sensory. None of these is a virtue word.

## Truths

1. The starter is 41 years old and was carried from a grandmother's village. Rival: a morning croissant shop. They laminate dough. They do not keep one mother culture for 41 years. This is truth 1.
2. The bake starts at 11pm and the door opens at 6am. Rival: a supermarket bakery that fires the ovens after dawn. They do not work the night shift. This is truth 2.
3. The baskets are empty by noon, and that emptiness is the night succeeding. Rival: a cafe that still wants pastry on the counter at 4pm. An empty case is their failure. This is truth 3.

## Emotion

Hunger.

A visitor tells a friend: "I watched one jar snap into a loaf and then into an empty basket, and the bench went quiet at noon."

## Concepts

1. The site is one starter's night route, where the jar, the loaf, and the bare basket are three bodies of the same culture, and scrolling snaps that round from the 11pm feed to the noon ghost. Rival: a supermarket bakery that bakes after dawn and still has loaves at 5pm. Their day has no village jar and an empty tray is a failure, not the ending. Keep.

2. The site is a flour clock with three teeth, 11pm, 6am, and noon, and the noon tooth is an empty ring because the bread itself is the hand that leaves. Rival: a 24-hour donut counter. A clock fits them, and they do not want the case empty. The empty tooth can be forced onto any shop that closes. FAIL.

   Replaced: The site is the rubber band climbing the jar, the menu written as millimetre ticks on the glass. Rival: a brewery marking a carboy with tape. The band and the ticks fit them. FAIL.

   Replaced again: The site is the lame's one cut across a 41-year culture, opening at 6am and gone, crust and all, by noon, and scrolling pulls the blade. Rival: a morning croissant bakery. They score dough, but they do not keep one village starter for 41 years, and a lamination is not a single cut across a mother culture. Keep.

3. The site is the road from the village crock to the Budapest bench, 41 years as one carry. Rival: a coffee roaster who imports a family lot. The carry fits them. FAIL.

   Replaced: The site is the oven mouth, shut at 11, bright at the bake, dark at noon. Rival: a pizza shop with a deck oven. The mouth fits them better. FAIL.

   Replaced again: The site is the couche's red guide thread, and the night is how far the loaf travels along that one stripe before the thread shows again at noon. Rival: a textile shop. A guide thread fits them better than a bakery. FAIL.

   Replaced again: The site is the heel left in the proofing basket, the only portrait, because every other loaf walked out before noon, and tapping the heel turns it into tomorrow's feed. Rival: a cheese counter with a last wedge. A last piece fits them, and they have no heel that becomes a 41-year starter again. Keep.

Three metaphors that survive the swap: the culture's three bodies, the lame's one cut, the heel that is fed back. Not three skins of a loaf illustration.

## Pick

World kept: Traveling object. One SVG, `#traveler`, three child groups, pinned at most 220vh, scrub between 0.6 and 1, captions of at most 18 words beside the object, a short practical footer after the pin.

World rejected: Worker in the room. A geometric baker would be any bakery's mascot, and the actor in this brief is the starter, not a person. The dealt motion is a detent, which would make a worker look like a stuck puppet. The footer-as-bed does not match a sell-out.

World rejected: The stack. A pile of loaves celebrates bread that is still here. Kovász's fact is that the pile is gone by noon. A stack of equal rounds would become a product grid in perspective. The title-on-a-spine fit belongs to records and books, not one living culture.

Concept picked: the night route of three bodies. The lame and the heel stay as objects inside that route (the delight, the last pose). They are not a second world.

This is the sentence. The visitor sees one round snap from fed jar, to scored loaf, to bare basket. Nothing from the clock concept or the worker concept is added on top.

## Visual system

Material: floured flax couche, because the loaves sleep in linen and flour sits in the weave. The page is that cloth, with the red guide thread woven at the left edge, not a paper theme and not a dark room.

Light: the bare bench bulb at 11pm, because that is the lamp over the feed and the door is shut. The cloth is mid olive, lit, not a black site. At the 6am detent the bulb goes out and a flour-colored door slit appears, because cold street light replaces the bulb. The slit is flat ink, not a glow.

Palette, five hexes, each from the cloth or the culture:

- `#8A917C` linen, because the couche outside the hottest part of the bulb is olive flax.
- `#F3EDE2` flour, because the score, the jar glass, and the morning door slit are dry flour.
- `#6E2A22` crust, because the baked loaf and the couche's red guide thread are the same baked red.
- `#1A1614` soot, because the second print pass is oven soot.
- `#E3B04A` starter, because a ripe culture and the bare bulb are the same dull gold. The gold is the culture and the bulb only, not a button color.

Type: display face Anton, file from `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Anton&family=Outfit:wght@400;600&display=swap`, because a screen clogs thin joints and the jar stamp is one heavy ink. Body face Outfit from that same URL, because the cloth labels are short written notes, not a second display. Body 18px. Display 133px (7.4rem at an 18px root), which is more than 4 times the body, because the stamp has to read across the bench the way a bag print does. Tracking on Anton `-0.03em`, because the face is already narrow and still needs the poster tightness. On a 700px screen, body 16px and display 68px, still more than 4 times.

Picture rule: kept, screenprint. Two passes of flat ink, soot misregistered 2px down and right of the crust or the starter, plus the starter gold as the third ink on the culture and the bulb only. No photographic texture. Large shapes, few of them. The picture depicts the jar, the banneton round, the scored loaf, and the empty basket. It does not depict a person.

Motion: kept, mechanical. `steps(6)` for the ink slam, hard cuts for the six story detents, 80 to 120ms a step, linear or steps, no overshoot, because the public facts are three hard times, 11pm, 6am, and noon, like an oven timer. The slow ferment is the change of body, not a glide. Menus and the step control are instant. One easing family only: steps and linear.

Sound: none.

## Objects

Content sits on these objects. No row of cards.

- Night feed, the starter and the shaped dough: `#state-jar`, `data-object="jar"`. Pose A: rubber band low, two bubbles, smaller round. Pose B: band snapped higher, four bubbles, round 12px larger, because the culture doubled. A soot chip on the lip, because the jar traveled. The number 41 is stamped on the glass.
- The loaf: `#state-loaf`, `data-object="loaf"`. Same center as the night round, so it is the same body. Pose A: crust circle, flour spiral, closed score. Pose B: the ear open along the score. A soot blister on the shoulder, because this loaf blistered and the jar did not.
- Sold out: `#state-empty`, `data-object="basket"`. Pose A: the spiral, a flour ghost ring, one small crust heel. Pose B: the heel gone. The spiral is an open arc on the right, not a perfect coil, because the basket is worn. The heel is much smaller than the loaf.
- Hours: `#clock`, `data-object="clock"`. Three teeth, not a bar. The 11pm tooth is the longest, because the night shift is the long work. The 6am tooth is mid. The noon tooth is the shortest, because nothing is left. The visitor's real hour wears a gold cap. The story detent wears a soot block. They differ in height and in which cap is on.
- The bulb: `#lamp`, `data-object="lamp"`. Gold and lit on the 11pm detents. Flour glass, filament dull, from 6am on. One cord, one circle.
- The delight, the lame: `#lame`, `data-object="lame"`. Handle soot, blade flour, smaller than the jar. A tap adds one cross score on the current body. It does not navigate.
- Practical lines: `#tag`, `data-object="tag"`. A sewn flour label, wider than the heel, stitches in crust thread, one tiny heel drawn on the patch. Footer copy sits on this patch only.

`#traveler` holds the jar, loaf, and basket groups. `#scene` carries `data-signature` on `#traveler`, `data-delight` on `#lame`, and the facts.

## Storyboard

Budget. One hero signature in the first viewport: the screenprinted jar and round, with the soot pass landing 2px off. One supporting journey: the same SVG pinned for 200vh, scrub 0.8, six hard detents. One hidden delight: the lame. One small surface: the sewn footer label. No other motion. Body text does not fade, rise, or stagger. No second scene, no cursor, no grain filter.

Techniques used, three: one object carries the story (pin, scrub 0.8, captions beside the object, at most 18 words), mechanical detents (`steps(6)`, 80 to 120ms), and the local hour read once (button verb, gold cap on the clock, one status line). The screenprint is the dealt picture rule, not a fourth technique.

Beats, including touch. Scroll is the pin. Touch uses the same scroll. Nothing here depends on hover. A fine pointer may nudge the lame 2px, and the tap still scores.

- 0 to 16% of the scroll: the jar and the gold round hold, band low, because 11pm is the feed and there is no crust yet. Touch: this frame is what loads, and a finger scroll leaves it the same way a wheel does.
- 16 to 33% of the scroll: the band snaps up and two bubbles appear, because the culture doubled. Touch: the snap is the scroll detent, not a hover.
- 33 to 50% of the scroll: the round snaps from starter gold to crust and the spiral turns to flour, bulb out, door slit on, because 6am opens the door on a baked loaf. Touch: scroll.
- 50 to 66% of the scroll: the ear snaps open along the score, because oven spring splits the cut. Touch: scroll.
- 66 to 83% of the scroll: the loaf snaps down to one heel in the spiral, because the morning took the rest. Touch: scroll.
- 83 to 100% of the scroll: the heel snaps away and the flour ghost ring remains, because they are gone by noon. Touch: scroll.

After the pin, only the sewn label. It does not animate.

The feed button stays inside `#scene` for the whole pin, so the action never leaves the first viewport's picture. At night it reads "Feed the night" and advances one detent in 120ms linear. In the morning it reads "Take the loaf" and jumps to the crust detent. After noon it reads "Come at 6" and jumps to the bare basket. Further clicks advance one detent, then the label.

Intro, first visit only: the soot pass steps from a 14px miss to the 2px miss in six steps of 80ms (480ms total). The headline and the button are visible the whole time. "Skip the slam" ends it. `sessionStorage` key `kovasz-ink` skips it on return. Reduced motion: no slam, already at 2px, no pin. Three instant buttons (11pm, 6am, noon) switch the same picture. The feed button still steps it.

Live hour, read once: 23 to 5 is the night shift, 6 to 11 is the open counter, 12 to 22 is past noon. It changes the button verb, the status line, and which clock tooth gets the gold cap. It does not reorder the story. The story always starts at the feed, because that is the route.

## Rhythm

The scene is a pinned poster: a stamp, one round, and the jar, with the action in a corner of the cloth. The reel under the pin is three drawings in a column, each a different body (jar, loaf, basket), words beside the drawing, not a card. The footer is one sewn label. None of these is heading, then text, then a card grid. The reel beats are not interchangeable with the footer or the poster.

## Removal

Build, then delete, the rising bubble loop inside the jar: bubbles traveling upward on a repeat, linear, staggered. It does not serve the concept. The culture is alive because it changes bodies on the detents, and a floating loop is a screensaver on the glass. Static bubbles of different sizes stay, because a fed starter has holes. A console log does not count. This is a real motion, removed.

## Voice

Headline, 3 words: "Gone by noon."

Subhead: "Kovász means the starter: 41 years old, carried from a grandmother's village, baked in Budapest from 11pm."

Button: "Feed the night." Morning: "Take the loaf." After noon: "Come at 6."

Empty state, on the bare basket: "The basket is bare. Flour is still warm."

Footer, on the sewn label: "Kovász, sample. Budapest. If you meet an empty basket, the night did its job."

Consent, on that same label: "No jar of your data. One culture is enough."

Loading, during the ink slam: "Hold on. The dough is still arguing with the oven."

The footer is the funny one. A stranger can tell it is this bakery: the name, the city, the empty basket as a success. The brand is marked sample on the red thread and on the label. No prices, no staff names, no village name, no quotes.

State captions, each under 18 words, beside the round:

- "11pm. The 41-year jar is fed, and the round starts its night."
- "6am. The door opens on a scored loaf still ticking as it cools."
- "Noon. The basket keeps the spiral. The loaf does not."

## Genericness

A similar brief is a corner boulangerie that sells pastries from morning until evening and wants a more creative site. For them I would also have reached for linen and a big round loaf, because that is the shared prop of bakeries. That part is the risk.

Changed: the round is not a mascot beside the menu. It is the middle body of a 41-year village jar, and the page's last picture is the basket with the loaf gone. An all-day pastry shop cannot end on an empty basket and call it the night doing its job. The red guide thread, the rubber band, the 11 / 6 / 12 teeth, and the heel are from this harvest, not from a pastry case. Escaping the cream-and-serif costume into black-and-acid, or into a particle field, would be a failed escape. This cloth stays olive, the inks stay crust, soot, flour, and starter gold, and the only motion left after removal is the detent of the night.
